HAZARD TYPES DESCRIPTIONS

Active Fault
A fracture or zone of fractures in the Earth's crust along which movement has occurred recently and is likely to occur again. Active faults can produce earthquakes and cause ground displacement, structural damage, and pose significant seismic hazards.

Earthquake-Induced Landslide (EIL)
A landslide triggered by the shaking of the ground during an earthquake. The seismic motion destabilizes slopes, causing soil, rock, and debris to move downhill.

Storm Surge
An abnormal rise of seawater caused by a storm, usually a tropical cyclone, that exceeds normal tidal levels, leading to coastal flooding and damage.

Tsunami
A series of large ocean waves generated by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides. Tsunamis travel at high speeds and can cause massive coastal inundation and destruction.

Rain-Induced Landslide (RIL)
A landslide triggered by heavy or prolonged rainfall that saturates the soil, reducing its stability and causing it to slide downhill.

Ground Shaking
The oscillatory motion of the Earth's surface during an earthquake. Its intensity depends on earthquake magnitude, distance from the epicenter, and local soil/rock conditions.

Liquefaction
Occurs when water-saturated, loose soils lose strength during strong earthquake shaking, causing the ground to behave like a liquid, which can lead to building tilting, sinking, or ground cracks.
